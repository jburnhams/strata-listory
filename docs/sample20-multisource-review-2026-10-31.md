# Twenty-entry review after approved multi-source queries

This assessment describes the investigation before output processing. A later books-only pilot processed entries 363, 388 and 413 with explicit review; see [book-output-pilot-2026-10-31.md](book-output-pilot-2026-10-31.md).

## Outcome

All 20 original entries were investigated using fresh Audible UK/US, Audnexus, Open Library, Hardcover, AudiobookDB and Apple Books results. Google Books was attempted on five entries and returned 429 for every request. Titles/authors were recovered from local evidence before searching. Search hits were manually reviewed; they were not accepted because a provider returned them.

**18/20 entries now have external corroboration of their identity.** These comprise **14 book-related entries representing 13 distinct original works**, one stand-up item, one short story and two Audible series episodes. The two Blagger's Guide radio episodes remain identified from local tags only, without external corroboration from the tested sources.

At the recording level, 4 entries have exact embedded/local ASINs corroborated by fresh Audible product records. Another 12 have supported recording candidates, often corroborated by narrator, language and measured duration at the correct parent-book level; this category includes provisional regional-release assignments. The remaining 4 have local-only recording identification. These categories describe the evidence, not automatic processing approvals. No books were confirmed, grouped, deleted, moved, approved or processed.

This supersedes the earlier Open Library-only assessment: The Noise of Time now has much stronger support from Audible and Apple, and the complete Sherlock Holmes parent folder has been measured against the actual collection listing.

## Which sources proved useful

- **Audible direct:** most useful recording source in this sample. Product details returned narrator, exact product ID, runtime, language, abridgement, overall/story/performance ratings and public customer reviews. UK/US product IDs and reissue dates must stay distinct. Some genuine product IDs are numeric; the current B-only ASIN filter would lose them.
- **Apple Books:** valuable corroboration. Its public product pages expose narrator, language and duration that were absent from Search API responses, plus some reviews/ratings. Nine of the ten fetched candidate pages fit the intended performance; the tenth was the rejected Spanish Hitch-22. Search result order is unsafe. [Example: Skunk Works](https://books.apple.com/gb/audiobook/skunk-works/id1791940807).
- **Hardcover:** useful work reviews, aggregate ratings, moods and tags. Manual selection was essential for English Outliers and complete Skunk Works author attribution. Contributor lists sometimes include narrators/editors/translators as authors; do not copy them blindly into final author fields.
- **Open Library:** useful work/year/subject corroboration; duplicates and bad years need field-level judgement. Not a recording-identity source.
- **Audnexus:** corroborated three of four embedded-ASIN items; one episode lookup returned 400. It shares Audible ancestry and must not count as an independent catalogue family.
- **AudiobookDB:** useful as another work-discovery source, including Riding in Cars with Boys. The current adapter discards detailed recording attributes, and many search hits were wrong. A response is not a verified match.
- **Google Books:** no usable evidence this run; all five requests returned 429. Do not count it as corroboration or keep hammering the endpoint. A configured project/key may be necessary before reliable use.
- **BBC Programme Index:** still the sensible source to test for the two radio episodes. Not queried in this approved seven-service comparison.
- **Goodreads:** not tested in this comparison; no Goodreads results or reviews are claimed.

## Recommended next implementation

1. Recover identity from tags and parent groups before provider searches; distinguish whole books, chapters, omnibus constituents, stories, stand-up and series episodes.
2. Associate all chapters with one recording and detect alternative representations, as with Hitch-22.
3. Add recording candidates using title/author, narrator, language, measured parent duration and identifiers. Preserve rejected candidates and reasons.
4. Separate the original work, recorded performance and regional/reissued product release. A matching performance does not establish the correct release ID, publisher or release date.
5. Preserve raw responses and field assertions permanently; select values with provenance. Keep work opinions and recording/performance opinions separate.
6. Only then calibrate automatic eligibility against this reviewed sample. The current assessor still has false blockers and missing checks; none of these research judgements silently authorises processing.
